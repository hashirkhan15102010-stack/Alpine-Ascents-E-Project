Mount Everest:
The Roof of the World

A MOUNTAIN WITH MANY NAMES

Above the clouds, rooted in culture

Rising 8,849 meters (29,032 feet) above sea level, Mount Everest is the highest point on Earth, earning its nickname, “the Roof of the World.” It sits on the border between Nepal and Tibet, in the Mahalangur Himal range of the Himalayas, a region of towering peaks, deep valleys, and glaciers that feed some of Asia’s great rivers. For the people who live in its shadow, the mountain is far more than a geographic landmark. Nepalis call it Sagarmatha, “Forehead of the Sky,” while Tibetans call it Chomolungma, “Goddess Mother of the World,” names that reflect the deep spiritual respect local communities hold for it. Today Everest attracts climbers, trekkers, scientists, and travelers from every continent, all drawn by the question: what does it feel like to stand at the top of the world?

A CENTURY OF EXPLORATION

A History of Ambition

For centuries, Everest was known only to the people who lived in its shadow. British surveyors identified it as the world’s tallest peak in the 1850s, and in the early 20th century several expeditions tried and failed to climb it. The 1924 attempt by George Mallory and Andrew Irvine, who disappeared high on the mountain, remains one of mountaineering’s great mysteries. On May 29, 1953, Edmund Hillary of New Zealand and Tenzing Norgay, a Sherpa from Nepal, became the first confirmed climbers to stand on top. Since then, thousands have reached the summit, including Junko Tabei, the first woman to do so, in 1975.

THE CLIMB

Adventure at Mount Everest

Mount Everest offers an unforgettable adventure filled with beauty, excitement, and challenges. Surrounded by snow-covered peaks, sparkling glaciers, and breathtaking Himalayan landscapes, climbers experience the true wonder of nature.

The journey is demanding, with freezing temperatures, strong winds, and low oxygen levels testing courage and endurance. After weeks of preparation and climbing, the summit opens onto a spectacular view of golden sunlight above the clouds.

FIRST LIGHT

Sunrise over the Himalayas

As the sun rises above the horizon, its golden rays shine on the snow-covered mountains, making them glow in shades of orange, pink, and gold.

Color washes across the sky while clouds gather around the Himalayan peaks. The fresh mountain air and peaceful surroundings make this view unforgettable.

A LIVING LANDSCAPE

Why Everest matters

As one of the world's most significant natural landmarks, Everest represents the beauty, power, and majesty of nature. It attracts climbers, researchers, and visitors from around the globe.

The mountain supports a remarkable Himalayan ecosystem, while its glaciers and snowfields are vital parts of regional water systems. Responsible tourism and mountaineering also support local communities.
